# Call-recording law (not legal advice)

:::warning This page is informational, not legal advice.
CallVault records **your own** calls for **personal** record-keeping. Whether — and how —
you may record, keep, or share a call depends on your jurisdiction and on whether you are
a participant in the conversation. If you plan to do anything beyond keeping a call for
your own reference, confirm the current local rules first, or consult someone who can.
:::

## The honest points

- **It depends on jurisdiction, not on the phone.** Many places let a participant record
  a conversation they are part of ("one-party consent"). Others require informing or
  getting consent from every party — especially before sharing or using a recording.
- **Recording ≠ sharing.** Keeping a personal record of a call you were on is generally
  different from publishing it, submitting it as evidence, or otherwise using it. The bar
  is usually higher for those.
- **Pakistan specifically.** Rules around interception and around *publishing or using*
  recordings can be involved, and differ from simply keeping a personal record of calls
  you took part in. Check before you use a recording beyond your own reference.

## What CallVault does and doesn't do

- CallVault is **transparent**: it shows a live banner while recording and keeps a
  persistent notification.
- CallVault does **not** play an audible "this call is being recorded" announcement. That
  is a product fact — the responsibility to inform the other party where your local law
  requires it is yours.
